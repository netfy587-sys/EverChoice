How to build an emergency fund, step by step

An emergency fund is money set aside for surprises such as medical bills, a job gap or urgent repairs. This guide explains how much to save and how to get there gradually.

Decide how much you need

A common guideline is three to six months of essential spending, such as rent, food, bills and loan payments. If your income is irregular, aim for the higher end. Start with a first target of one month.

Open a separate account

Keep the fund apart from daily spending so you are not tempted to use it. A savings account that is easy to access but not linked to your debit card works well.

Automate small, regular transfers

Set up an automatic transfer on payday. Even a small fixed amount adds up, and automation removes the need to decide each month.

Know what counts as an emergency

Medical costs, urgent home or vehicle repairs and loss of income qualify. Sales, holidays and planned purchases do not. Write your own short rules in advance.

Rebuild after you use it

If you spend from the fund, treat refilling it as the next priority before you increase other spending.

Where the extra savings can come from

  • Review subscriptions you no longer use.
  • Move any bonus or tax refund straight to the fund.
  • Round up spare change into savings.
  • Pause the fund once it is full and redirect the money to other goals.

The 50/30/20 budget

A simple starting framework for splitting take-home income. Adjust it to your life.

50% Needs
Rent, food, utilities, transport and minimum debt payments.
30% Wants
Eating out, hobbies, streaming and holidays.
20% Future
Emergency fund, extra debt payments and long-term saving.

Where to keep the money

Choose safety and easy access over high returns. A savings account you can withdraw from within a day or two is a common choice. Some people split the fund: one month in an instant-access account and the rest in an account with slightly better interest that still lets them withdraw.

  • Avoid locking money into long fixed terms.
  • Avoid assets whose value can drop when you need cash.
  • Check whether your deposits are covered by a protection scheme in your country.

Common mistakes to avoid

Small habits can quietly drain a fund.

  • Dipping into it for sales or holidays.
  • Waiting to save until you can save a large amount.
  • Keeping it in the same account as daily spending.
  • Forgetting to refill it after use.
  • Not reviewing the target when your costs rise.

Frequently asked questions

Should I pay off debt or build an emergency fund first?

Many people build a small starter fund first, then pay down high-interest debt. Your situation may differ, so consider speaking to a qualified financial adviser. This guide is general information, not financial advice.

Where should I keep an emergency fund?

Somewhere safe and easy to reach, such as a savings account. It should not be invested in something that can lose value quickly.
